I used synthetic data to assess the impact of increasing numbers of uncertain-fate nests and misclassified nests on estimates of daily survival rate (DSR) of nests. Uncertain nest fates and misclassified nest fates were not fixed values but rather emergent properties of the modeled storm behavior. This is an example of a "virtual ecologist" model, because there is a modeled observer and the "true" underlying values are known, allowing me to estimate the bias in nest survival estimates that results from different methods.

### The virtual ecologist model has two submodels:

* <span style="color: #2a4fb5; font-weight: bold;">The nest creation submodel.</span>

    Randomly chooses initiation date from a real distribution of SW Louisiana nest initiation dates. Randomly samples a negative binomial distribution to get number of days until nest failure (probability = 1-DSR). If number of days exceeds incubation length, nest hatches.

* <span style="color: #2a4fb5; font-weight: bold;">The observer submodel.</span>

    Ability to correctly classify nest fate decreases over time following a simple exponential decay function. Storms increase the rate of evidence decay, making it more likely that observer will have nests with uncertain fate. 

    For uncertain-fate nests during storms, observer has a choice: 

    * <span style="color: #7A67EE; font-weight: bold;">Observer 1</span> marks these nest fates "unknown" 

    * <span style="color: #40E0D0; font-weight: bold;">Observer 2</span> marks these nest fates "flooded"

    This means some fraction of nests are either excluded because fate is unknown, or are misclassified because they did not truly flood.
